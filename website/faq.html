For most athletes, yes — provided you film your main lifts. You lose the hands-on cueing; you keep the programming, the video review and the testing. Athletes who need technique rebuilt from scratch do better in person, at least for the first block. I'll tell you honestly which camp you're in.

A barbell, plates, a rack and somewhere to run covers almost everything. If you're missing something, I build the block around what you actually have — tell me at the intake stage rather than buying kit first.

Programming-only is a one-off fee; online and 1:1 coaching are monthly. Exact pricing depends on session frequency and block length — starting points are on the Services page, and I quote you properly once I know what you're training for.

No rolling contract you can't leave. Coaching is billed monthly with 30 days' notice to stop. I do ask for a minimum of one full block — around twelve weeks — because anything shorter can't be meaningfully tested, and testing is the whole point.

Strength markers usually move within four to six weeks. Speed and jump numbers take longer, which is why the first formal retest sits at eight to twelve weeks. Anyone promising a transformation faster than that is selling you something.
